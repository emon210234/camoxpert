# CamoXpert: Adaptive Camouflaged Object Detection via Boundary-Aware Mamba-MoE Networks

## Abstract
Camouflaged object detection (COD) remains difficult because targets are intentionally blended with their surroundings, making conventional contrast cues unreliable. These scenes suppress texture and contour saliency, forcing models to reason over weak, noisy signals while preserving fine boundaries. The challenge is amplified by intrinsic similarity, disruptive coloration, and scale ambiguity, which erase boundaries and scatter weak clues across feature hierarchies. To this end, we propose CamoXpert, an adaptive pipeline that first aggregates global context through Cross-Scale Selective Scan (CS-SSM), then applies Adaptive Scale Weighting (ASW) to emphasize the most informative resolutions, before a Spectral Gated MoE (SG-MoE) selectively refines frequency-specific details; a Boundary Refinement Module (BRM) subsequently sharpens the invisible edges that remain ambiguous. The model is optimized with a multi-component CamouflageDetectionLoss that jointly enforces pixel accuracy, region consistency, structural fidelity, and edge supervision. Extensive evaluations on standard COD benchmarks demonstrate that CamoXpert consistently surpasses recent state-of-the-art methods, delivering stronger boundary integrity and more reliable detection under extreme background similarity.

## Introduction
Camouflage is a biological survival strategy in which organisms conceal themselves through structural and physiological adaptations that mimic their environments [1]. While generic object detection (GOD) [2, 3] and salient object detection (SOD) [4, 5] rely on strong contrast, camouflaged object detection (COD) targets entities that suppress their visual presence [6]. Such targets can be extremely small, highly similar to background textures, or partially obscured, requiring recognition of subtle and ambiguous cues rather than clear semantic separation [7]. COD is therefore critical in safety, surveillance, medical analysis, agriculture, wildlife monitoring, and defense scenarios where hidden objects must be localized despite deliberate visual deception.

Biologically, camouflage manifests through texture mimicry, background matching, and disruptive coloration that breaks an object’s contour [6]. These mechanisms yield boundary uncertainty and the well-known “bleeding edge” phenomenon in which foreground and background blend indistinguishably. Computationally, this results in severe challenges: models must recover long-range context to validate object presence while retaining fine detail for boundary localization, and they must generalize to novel appearances despite limited annotated data and real-time constraints [1, 20].

The invisible edge problem also exposes a limitation of many current CNN and ViT-based solutions: static multi-scale integration. As summarized in the thesis narrative, most state-of-the-art models aggregate features with fixed convolutional or attention summations, applying the same fusion logic to every scene. This static matching often hallucinates foreground in noisy textures or overlooks objects when local cues are ambiguous. Although transformers provide global receptive fields, their quadratic complexity limits efficient modeling of high-resolution dependencies, which are essential in COD [23].

To address these issues, we introduce CamoXpert (V12), a dynamic architecture grounded in the current codebase. Multi-scale features are first contextualized by Cross-Scale Selective Scan (CS-SSM), which performs linear-complexity global reasoning across scales. Adaptive Scale Weighting (ASW) then modulates the contribution of large, medium, and small resolutions per image, enabling content-aware fusion. The Spectral Gated MoE (SG-MoE) leverages frequency cues to activate specialized experts for high-frequency boundaries versus low-frequency regions, and the Boundary Refinement Module (BRM) explicitly sharpens ambiguous contours before prediction. Training uses a CamouflageDetectionLoss that combines BCE, IoU, SSIM, and edge supervision to reinforce both region integrity and boundary precision.

**Main Contributions**
- **Adaptive Scale Weighting (ASW):** a content-aware multi-scale weighting module that replaces static fusion and highlights the most informative resolutions for each image.
- **Boundary Refinement Module (BRM):** an edge-guided refinement stage that targets the invisible edge problem and improves boundary localization in disruptive camouflage.
- **Spectral Gated MoE with CS-SSM context:** a hybrid decoder that couples cross-scale selective scan with frequency-gated expert refinement for robust global-local reasoning.
- **CamouflageDetectionLoss:** a multi-component objective (BCE + IoU + SSIM + edge) that aligns region accuracy with structural and boundary fidelity.

## Related Works
### Traditional COD methods
Early COD approaches relied on handcrafted cues to expose subtle foreground–background differences. Texture-based segmentation methods such as TSMA analyze “textons” and filter-response statistics to separate textured regions [8]. Intensity-driven methods like CBDCE extend this to 3D shape cues by distinguishing convex and concave regions, improving robustness to scale and illumination changes [9]. Additional classical pipelines employ wavelet-based texture smoothing [10], adaptive background modeling and region grouping for surveillance videos [11], and optical-flow reasoning to handle dynamic scenes [12]. Color and edge integration methods further refine detection in outdoor scenarios by combining chromatic, gradient, and intensity evidence [22]. Despite their utility in controlled settings, these techniques struggle when camouflage suppresses visible differences or when images are low resolution and cluttered.

### Deep-learning COD methods
Recent COD models leverage deep representations to capture both global context and local detail. Ref-COD introduces referring images and R2CNet’s dual-branch design to guide segmentation of specific camouflaged targets [25]. ZoomNeXt simulates human-like zooming using a triplet-scale encoder and MHSIU-based feature fusion, with RGPU refinement and uncertainty-aware supervision [7]. ProMaC exploits MLLM hallucinations to generate prompts for training-free segmentation with SAM, demonstrating strong cross-domain performance [26]. FSPNet employs transformer-based feature shrinkage with non-local token enhancement to recover subtle cues [16], while Spider unifies context-dependent tasks through group prompts and concept filtering [27]. HGINet models hierarchical graph interactions and confidence-weighted fusion to mine aligned semantic cues and improve segmentation accuracy [28]. These deep models advance COD substantially but often incur high computational cost and depend on large, clean annotations [23].

In contrast, CamoXpert V12 targets the invisible edge problem and static fusion limitations by combining CS-SSM for linear-complexity global reasoning, ASW for content-adaptive scale selection, SG-MoE for frequency-aware expert routing, and BRM for explicit boundary refinement, all trained with a multi-component CamouflageDetectionLoss. This design directly addresses the intrinsic similarity of camouflaged scenes while maintaining efficient, adaptive processing across scales.
